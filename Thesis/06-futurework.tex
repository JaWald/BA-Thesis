\chapter{Future Work}
\label{sec:future-work}
\paragraph{Scenario 1} Bevy's partial vectorization remains a subject of further inspection as it poses the question whether a \ac{SoA}-like component layout \cite{bevy_contiguous} would allow for full vectorization and not be overshadowed by the increased overhead from component management. Additionally, the processor used for this thesis only allowed for the usage of \qty{256}{\bit} \texttt{ymm} registers. A processor featuring AVX-512 would allow for even broader registers and potentially increase the gap between the data-oriented and object-oriented architectures.

\paragraph{Scenario 2} 
The source of the spikes in the execution time of the \ac{PC} architecture remains open and could be investigated, as it is not explained by the cache conflict misses relevant for the \ac{AoS} architecture. Additionally, this thesis only scaled the payload to \qty{20}{\kibi\byte} which primarily surfaced L1 conflict-miss effects. Increasing the payload to higher values may reveal additional effects attributable to the L2 and L3 caches. 

\paragraph{Scenario 3} 
The \texttt{tcache} cutoff is only one of many thresholds. Thus, other limits such as the fastbin/smallbin boundary or the \texttt{mmap} threshold could be visualized through further scaling the payload. Further, different allocators could be investigated to reveal whether the \ac{PC} architecture severely underperformed due to the specific allocator used in this thesis or whether it is a general problem with this architecture. 

\paragraph{General}
While this thesis isolated effects, a combination of the three scenarios could reveal whether the measured effects interact with one another and, e.g., add up. Additionally, all benchmark runs were performed on a single core. \ac{ECS} and \ac{SoA} layouts usually scale differently when using multi-threading. Wingqvist et al. \cite{wingqvist2022evaluating} investigated a multi-threaded build and could serve as a further basis.
